Service bits, and a deliberate deviation

Two service-bit filter prefixes are supported:

  • x10000009 — NODE_NETWORK|NODE_WITNESS|NODE_BLAKE2B
  • x10000408

A bare hostname query, with no filter prefix, also has NODE_BLAKE2B forced on. The seed therefore never returns a non-fork node, under any query. This is a deliberate deviation from stock bitcoin-seeder behaviour.

The reason: the fork shares port 8333 with the much larger SHA256d network. An unfiltered answer would be composed mostly of peers that are useless to a fork node.

How to use it

Query the seed directly:

dig +short x10000009.seed.thelionpool.org

Or point a node at it:

bitcoind -seednode=seed.thelionpool.org

Incident: served set collapse, 2026-09-20

For roughly 30 hours up to 2026-09-20 the seed's served set collapsed from 2967 nodes to 158, and DNS answers fell to 1–2 addresses.

Cause

The crawler selects nodes from a single FIFO shared with the whole SHA256d network. That queue held approximately 257,000 addresses while only about 400 of them were fork nodes. A full cycle took about 2.3 hours.

The seeder rates a node "good" using a decaying counter (tau = 2 hours) that requires more than 2 observations. That means a node must be polled at least every tau*ln2 = 83 minutes to stay rated. At a 2.3 hour cycle, fork nodes decayed out of the served set while still being perfectly reachable.

Fix

A second rotation queue was added for nodes confirmed to advertise NODE_BLAKE2B, given a fixed 5% share of crawl picks. Enrolment requires a successful handshake, so gossip-advertised service bits alone do not qualify a node.

Result

Fork nodes polled within the 83-minute window went from 46 of 396 to 291 of 379, and DNS answers returned to a full packet.

The fix is in the public repository linked above.

Policy compliance

Against doc/dnsseed-policy.md:

  • Results are crawled live. They are not biased or filtered per requester.
  • Each answer is a randomly selected subset of the currently-good set, so two queries will usually differ. No requester or group of requesters is singled out to receive different results.
  • TTL is 3600 seconds, comfortably above the 60s minimum.
  • DNS query logs are not retained or shared. Only operational crawl data is kept.
  • Crawl data — which nodes were reachable — may be published. It is not made more complete by biasing connectivity.
  • A contact address is published here, and is also in the zone's SOA RNAME.

No compliance is claimed beyond the points listed above.
